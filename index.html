01 — About

Research with a systems perspective.

Connecting building performance, intelligent control and the energy infrastructure around us.

Buildings
as energy assets.

My academic interests sit at the intersection of building physics, HVAC engineering, energy flexibility and control.

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Energy and Process Engineering at NTNU, working within the Sustainable Energy Systems research environment.

My research focuses on modelling and coordinating building energy systems so that heating, cooling, on-site renewables and thermal storage can respond to both building needs and external energy signals.

I am particularly interested in practical approaches that bridge detailed building simulation and advanced control — including model predictive control — while accounting for existing equipment and real operating conditions.

02 — Research

What I am exploring.

A research direction focused on designing and operating building energy systems as active participants in future energy systems.

⌂

Building & HVAC modelling

Developing and integrating models of building envelopes, ventilation, heating and cooling systems to understand whole-building performance.

↔

Energy flexibility

Investigating how buildings can shift, modulate and coordinate energy use while maintaining comfort and operational requirements.

⌁

Advanced control

Exploring rule-based strategies and model predictive control for coordinated operation of building energy systems.

♨

Thermal storage

Studying the role of building mass and dedicated thermal storage in balancing heat production, demand and energy prices.

☀

Renewables & integration

Considering on-site renewable generation, heat pumps and storage as integrated parts of building energy systems.

⚡

Grid interaction

Understanding the interfaces between buildings, district heating networks and electricity grids.
